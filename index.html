Free Site Assessment

Every quote begins with an on-site assessment rather than a satellite estimate. We check roof orientation, pitch, shading across the day, structural condition and the capacity of your existing electrical panel, then size the system against twelve months of your actual bills. The assessment costs you nothing and you keep the findings whether or not you proceed.

Licensed and Insured

Design, permitting, utility interconnection, installation and commissioning are all handled in-house. That gives you one point of accountability for the whole project instead of a chain of subcontractors, and it means the permitting and interconnection paperwork is tracked by the same people who designed the system.

Written Workmanship Warranty

Panels, inverters and installation workmanship are each covered by separate terms, and we set all of them out in writing at handover. That includes our own warranty on the installation itself and on every roof penetration, so responsibility is clear if anything needs attention later.


Production Monitoring Included

Every system is commissioned with string-level monitoring so output can be checked against what the design predicted. An underperforming section shows up in the monitoring data early, rather than being discovered indirectly through an unexpectedly high electricity bill several months later.

About Bahawalnagar

Bahawalnagar (Punjabi, Urdu: بہاول نگر), is the capital city of Bahawalnagar District in the Punjab province of Pakistan. The city of Bahawalnagar is the headquarter of the district and tehsil as well. It is the 39th largest city of Pakistan by population according to the 2023 census. Old name of Bahawalnagar was Rojanwali/Ubbha. It was named Bahawalnagar in 1904 after Bahawal Khan V, the ruler of the Bahawalpur state comprising the present districts of Bahawalpur, Bahawalnagar and Rahim Yar Khan. It is 262 km (163 mi) south of Lahore and about 175 km (109 mi) east of Bahawalpur. River Sutlej passes on the northern side at a distance of about 6 miles.

Bahawalnagar, originally known as Rojhanwali, was a small settlement consisting of a few huts and shacks. This village Rojhanwali is still in existence at a distance of about 1.5 miles north of the Railway Station. Bahawalnagar is located at the historic road constructed by Sher Shah Suri.

In the year 1895, a railway station was established and was named Rojhanwali Railway Station. It was a junction between Bahawalnagar and Bhatinda at a distance of 113 miles, now linking branch lines Amrooka and Fort Abbas. Before partition it was a very busy junction. Trains used to run from Karachi to Delhi and vice versa via Bahawalnagar.

In 1904, when Nawab Mohammad Bahawal Khan Abbasi IV, who ruled over Bahawalpur State, visited Rojhanwali, the people asked that with his permission they wanted to rename the city after the Nawab. The Nawab allowed them with pleasure and since then the city has been named Bahawalnagar. With the advent of railways, 40 to 50 shops with a population of 100 dwelling units developed at the existing railway bazaar. With the introduction of the canal system, it has become a fertile area. The real achievement was the Fordwah Canal, which brought almost the entire district under the command of the irrigation system.

In the year 1900 to 1901, the first police station post was established, and in 1902 to 1903 the first primary school was established. In the year 1906 a panchayat committee consisting of nominated members was composed. In 1914 Bahawalnagar was recommended as a Tehsil and the police post Jweka was shifted to Bahawalnagar. A regular municipal committee was constituted in the year 1922.

The literacy rate of Bahawalnagar is less than 25% and the unemployment rate is much higher than 25%. Government owned institutions are offering their services to increase the literacy rate. The sub-campus of Islamia University is also located in Bahawalnagar.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do solar panels still produce power on cloudy days?

Yes, though at reduced output. Panels respond to available light rather than direct sun alone, so an overcast day typically produces a fraction of clear-sky output rather than nothing at all. Annual production estimates already account for typical local cloud cover, which is why sizing is done against a full year of data rather than a single season.

What happens to the system if I sell the house?

An owned system generally transfers with the property, and documentation matters at that point: the original design, permitting records, warranty terms and monitoring history are what a buyer or their inspector will ask for. We provide that documentation at handover, so keep it with your property records rather than only in an email inbox.

Do my panels keep working during a power outage?

Not by default. A standard grid-tied system shuts down during an outage for the safety of utility line workers, so it will not power the property even in daylight. Backup during outages requires battery storage with the appropriate switching equipment, which is specified separately from the array itself.
